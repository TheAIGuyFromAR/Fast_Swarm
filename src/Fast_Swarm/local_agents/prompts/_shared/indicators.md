# Technical Indicator Dictionary

## Momentum Oscillators

### RSI (Relative Strength Index)
Measures the ratio of recent upward vs downward price movement.
- **0-30:** OVERSOLD - Recent price action predominantly downward. Sellers dominated.
- **30-70:** NEUTRAL - Mixed price action. Neither buyers nor sellers dominating.
- **70-100:** OVERBOUGHT - Recent price action predominantly upward. Buyers dominated.

### Stochastic (%K)
Where current close sits relative to the high-low range over N periods.
- **0-20:** Price near the BOTTOM of its recent range.
- **20-80:** Price in the MIDDLE of its recent range (neutral).
- **80-100:** Price near the TOP of its recent range.

### CCI (Commodity Channel Index)
How far price has deviated from its statistical mean.
- **Below -100:** Price significantly below recent average (potential reversal).
- **-100 to +100:** Normal range.
- **Above +100:** Price significantly above recent average (potential reversal).

## Trend Indicators

### ADX (Average Directional Index)
Measures trend STRENGTH, not direction!
- **0-20:** WEAK/NO TREND - Price moving sideways or choppy. Mean reversion works.
- **20-40:** DEVELOPING TREND - Trend is forming.
- **40-60:** STRONG TREND - Clear directional movement.
- **60+:** VERY STRONG TREND - Rare, powerful moves.

### MACD
Relationship between fast (12) and slow (26) exponential moving averages.
- **MACD Line > Signal Line:** Momentum increasing (bullish).
- **MACD Line < Signal Line:** Momentum decreasing (bearish).
- **Crossover above:** Momentum turning up.
- **Crossover below:** Momentum turning down.

### Supertrend Direction
Trend-following indicator using volatility bands.
- **+1:** Bullish trend (price above support band).
- **-1:** Bearish trend (price below resistance band).

## Volatility Indicators

### ATR (Average True Range)
Average range of price movement per period. Higher = more volatile.

### Bollinger Bands
Statistical bands around a moving average.
- **Price at upper band:** ~2 standard deviations above average.
- **Price at lower band:** ~2 standard deviations below average.
- **Narrow bands:** Low volatility. **Wide bands:** High volatility.

## Volume Indicators

### OBV (On-Balance Volume)
Cumulative volume that adds on up days, subtracts on down days.
- **Rising OBV:** More volume on up days (accumulation).
- **Falling OBV:** More volume on down days (distribution).

---

## KEY CONCEPT: Indicators EXPLAIN, Not Compound

When you see oversold RSI + oversold Stoch + bearish MACD + bearish Supertrend, these are NOT four separate reasons to avoid.

They are ONE story: "Price has fallen significantly and is now at depressed levels."
- Bearish MACD explains WHY price fell
- Bearish Supertrend confirms the down move happened
- Oversold RSI/Stoch show WHERE price ended up (at lows)

The question: "Now that price is at these lows, what happens next?" NOT "How many bearish signals?"

## Indicator Combinations

### Confirming vs Diverging Signals

When multiple indicators point the same direction, they are **confirming**:
- RSI oversold + Stochastic oversold + Price at lower Bollinger Band = Multiple measures agree price has fallen significantly.

When indicators disagree, they are **diverging**:
- Price making new lows but RSI making higher lows = Momentum is not confirming the price move. This is a BULLISH divergence suggesting reversal.

### Trend vs Mean-Reversion Context

The same indicator reading has different implications depending on market context:

**In strong trends (high ADX > 40):**
- Oscillators can stay at extremes for extended periods
- BUT extreme oversold (Stoch < 10) often still bounces

**In ranging markets (low ADX < 20):**
- Oscillators reliably revert from extremes
- Oversold + low ADX = high probability mean reversion
